Your public address can change (a router restart, for one). The link in the app is refreshed when it does; send the new one if you notice listeners can't connect.

If your connection can't be opened
  • Ask your internet provider for a public IPv4 address, or to take you off carrier-grade NAT. Some do it free on request; some charge a little.
  • Use a ZeroTier network. 9GSound already works over ZeroTier: put the station and listeners on the same network and they connect with no port forwarding at all.
  • Mobile data can't host a station. Mobile carriers don't accept inbound connections. Put the phone on Wi-Fi.
  • A different Wi-Fi. A venue or a friend's connection that does have a public address will work with the steps above.
How this test works

Your browser asks this page's server to open a connection back to the address it sees you at, on the port you give. It can only ever connect to your own address, never one you type in. It then reads the first few bytes: a 9GSound station introduces itself with its name. Nothing is stored.

Whether a closed port was rejected or just silent is inferred from how quickly the connection failed, so treat that distinction as a strong hint rather than proof.

Stations on air

    9GSound companion. A station that is running and reachable is listed in the directory above (its name, public address and port) until it stops. Opening a port exposes your station to the whole internet; 9GSound limits connections and ignores anything that doesn't introduce itself properly, but only run a station on a network and phone you're comfortable exposing.
